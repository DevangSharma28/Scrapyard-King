# Scrap Yard King — Art Direction and Visual Overhaul

Target: a colourful industrial toy world in premium cartoon 3D: busy scrapyard, satisfying destruction, mobile tycoon.
Reference: the two owner screenshots (red crusher with a full hopper, yellow sorter, colour-coded bins, market stall
with green cash, chunky workers, big scrap piles along the walls).

Rules:
- **No Kenney visuals.** Every model is built in-house with ArtKit (procedural ProBuilder-style modelling). Kenney CC0
  audio is still used.
- **Built by builders.** Art is generated by `AgentScripts/Art_*.cs` and is idempotent: re-running a builder updates
  meshes, materials and prefabs in place and keeps their GUIDs.
- **Gameplay untouched.** Pads, hoppers, output points, colliders, labels and routes keep their positions. The only
  runtime additions are presentation components: `ScrapDamageStages`, `AmbientMotion`, `AmbientPath`,
  `UpgradeVisualTiers.objects` and `MachineVisuals.glow/outputBurst/idleSpin`.

## Pipeline (ArtKit, `Assets/_Project/Scripts/Editor/ArtKit`, Editor-only asmdef)

- `MeshKit`: rounded/chamfered boxes (24-vertex chamfer; rounder 2-segment boxes for characters), lathe shapes
  (cylinders, cones, domes, barrels, tyres, hoppers; low side counts are faceted), tori, bevelled prisms (car bodies),
  planes, a transform stack, box-projected UVs in metres, and three outputs: multi-material, compact or palette mesh.
- `ArtMaterials`: the palette and stylized URP Lit materials with procedural tileable detail textures.
- `ArtPalette`: shared flat-colour atlas (props, loose items) so whole clusters batch with one material.
- `PaletteBaker`: merges every flat-colour submesh of a model into one palette submesh (colour RGB, smoothness alpha,
  glow in an emission map). Textured materials (hazard stripes, brick, belt, concrete, wood) and per-instance
  recolours (bin and stall paint) stay separate.
- `ToyRig`: segmented toy characters baked into one skinned mesh (rigid weights on 12 joints) with a per-character
  palette material, plus the generated clips (idle, walk, run, tool hold, cut with a spinning blade, cheer) and the
  controllers `AC_ToyPlayer` / `AC_ToyWorker` / `AC_ToyCustomer` (same Speed / Cutting / Happy parameters as before).
- `ArtIcons`: renders any prefab into a 256 px card icon.

## Builders (run in this order after the M-builders)

1. `Art_Characters.Build` (`Preview(dir)` renders a lineup): clips, controllers, player + cutter tiers, workers,
   customers, worker / cutter / backpack / boots icons.
2. `Art_Scrap.Build` (`Preview(dir)`): damage VFX, every scrap prefab (in place), scrap palette, loose pieces, icons.
3. `Art_Machines.All` (`Prefabs`, `Scene`, `Icons`; `Preview(dir)`): stations, conveyors, bin/market colours, icons.
4. `Art_VFX.Build`: shared effects, particle sprites, new bursts wired into existing slots.
5. `Art_World.All` (`Props`, `Scene`, `Lighting`): prop prefabs, ground, decals, walls, mounds, clusters, backdrop,
   ambient life, NavMesh rebake, lighting and post.

M7 content is built with the same kit by `M7_Build` (run after the art pass): the operator console, the Truck Dock
(kerb, bollards, floodlights, a teal flatbed truck with spinning wheels and a cargo grid), the Giant Truck (a 10 m
mining dump truck: 16 detachable parts, three damage stages, its own baked palette `GiantPalette`), the giant's
landing zone and counter sign, and the HUD boss bar. `Art_Characters` holds the three new worker looks (Operator:
white hard hat, teal bib, ear muffs; Seller: red apron and mustache; Loader: beanie, purple vest, bare arms) and the
`Toy_Work` clip (both hands busy at chest height) behind the worker controller's `Working` bool.

M8 adds the Dockyard with `M8_Build`: a 28 m cargo ship (navy hull, white bridge, red funnel, three bays of deck
containers; it rolls and heaves on `AmbientMotion`), two ship-to-shore cranes (blue portal, yellow twin-girder boom,
a trolley that carries a container out over the ship and back on `AmbientPath`), bollards, the quay wall with fender
tyres, Gate 4 (blue and white bars) and the "coming soon" slip. Ship and cranes are permanent scenery behind the
plant's east wall, so they are the thing to want long before the gate can be bought.

Re-running an M-builder that regenerates prefabs (`M2_Build.Prefabs`, `M4_Build.Prefabs`, `M5_Build.Prefabs`,
`M6_Build.Prefabs`) brings Kenney models back; re-run the Art builders after it.

## UI kit (2026-10-06)

The HUD and world UI wear the owner's painted kit: chunky industrial panels (cream bodies, yellow hazard frames, blue
headers, dark plates), glossy buttons and painted icons. Sources are in `Assets/UI`; `UI_Build.Kit` cuts them into
`Art/UI/Kit` and `UI_Build.Apply` dresses the existing UI in place (`Docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` 8c has the kit table and
the rules).

Where each piece went:
- **Top bar:** currency on dark bars with the cash bundle and the gem; yard level as a number on the gold star, XP in
  a framed bar.
- **Task banner:** cream bar, framed progress bar, green check button when done.
- **Upgrade panel:** dark panel with the yellow hazard header, the title on a yellow plate that straddles the top
  edge, red close button. Cards: blue header with the name, cream body with the rendered icon, green buy button with
  the savings fill, dark lock pill with the padlock.
- **Boss bar:** the dark hazard title plate is the frame; health and its trail run in the slot.
- **Tiles:** hire and gate tiles are dark plates with yellow corner brackets (the brackets take the ready / locked
  tint); the upgrade tile shows the gear-and-arrow icon, boost pads the painted bolt.
- **Station labels:** dark name bar, yellow level pill, cream counter chip, white pill for the status tag (tinted).
- **Customer bubbles:** cream bubble, framed patience bar, green check when served. The plain bubble was kept over
  the kit's blue-headed speech bubble: the order icon and number need the whole bubble to read at a glance.
- **Loading screen:** the portrait painting (cropped evenly to fill any aspect), the title logo popping in, a hazard
  plate with the bar. **App icon:** `MainAppIcon`.

Text stays GROBOLD (outline material on dark and coloured surfaces, plain navy on cream).

## Revamp models (from 2026-10-06)

The revamp brief asks for hero machines: every major machine recognisable from across the yard, with moving parts, and
bigger machines more impressive than smaller ones (Crusher < Splitter < Furnaces < Press < truck loading < crane <
ship). Built so far:

- **Metal Splitter** (`Art_Machines.Sorter`, prefab `Stations/Sorter`): 5.6 m wide yellow body, red separator drum on
  a gantry, four spinning rollers on the screen deck, a centre hopper, and four gates in a row on the south face. Each
  gate has a hood, a chute and a plate in its material's colour with the item icon, and a paddle that strokes every
  cycle. Colour code, used on gates, belts' bins and items alike: iron grey `(0.55, 0.60, 0.68)`, aluminum pale blue
  `(0.72, 0.84, 0.95)`, copper orange `(0.95, 0.52, 0.22)`, steel dark blue `(0.30, 0.36, 0.50)`. Keep it for every
  later stage (furnaces, refined metals, bars): the player follows a metal through the factory by its colour.
- **Aluminum** (strapped bale of crushed cans) and **steel** (short I-beam): `R1_Build.Prefabs`, meshes in
  `Art/Meshes/Toy/R1_Items.asset`. Item metallic stays at 0.3 (icon studio rule).

- **Furnace battery** (`Art_Machines.Furnace`, prefabs `Stations/Furnace`, `Furnace_Copper`, `Furnace_Aluminum`,
  `Furnace_Steel`): one builder, four silhouettes on the same compact footprint (2.8 m wide, steel 3.3 m). Iron: a
  charcoal dome with orange bands and one tall chimney. Copper: a red box with twin stacks and copper pipes down both
  sides. Aluminum: a tall silver kiln with pale-blue bands and a side duct. Steel: a wide dark-blue stepped blast
  furnace with three red-capped stacks. Front of each: hopper shelf left, glowing mouth (its own colour, own
  material so the palette baker leaves it alone), output chute right. Each has a fan, a bellows, a beacon, an extra
  stack at Lv.3 and gold trim at Lv.5. A bought-later slot shows a hazard-posted foundation with a sign in the metal's
  colour. The ingots (aluminum pale, steel blue) reuse the iron ingot's mesh.

- **Industrial Press** (`Art_Machines.Press`, prefab `Stations/Press`): a hydraulic press on four chrome columns over
  a dark hazard-banded bed. Blue crown with bolts and two gauges, the cylinder and hoses on top, a ram that slams onto
  the anvil each cycle, a billet that glows while it works, a flywheel on the east side, feed tray west, output slide
  south. Kept under 5 m and open between the columns, because it stands south of the hall's walkway. Lv.3 adds two
  yellow accumulator tanks, Lv.5 gold trim.
- **Bars** (`R3_Build.Prefabs`, mesh `R3_Items/Bar`): one trapezoid bar with a gold stamp plate, in the four metal
  colours, shinier than the ingots. The **Bar Storage** is the storage bin in dark steel with yellow trim.

Still to build to the brief's bar: truck loading, a working crane, the port and ship cycle, a denser
Heavy Scrap Yard with vehicle variants, and a wear / decal pass over the environment. Each belongs to its revamp step
in `Docs/QUALITY_PLAN.md`.

## Buy tiles and the Claw Crane (2026-10-06)

- **Buy tiles** (`UI_Build.PurchaseTile`, prefab `Tiles/Tile_Purchase`): the owner found the old tile "weird" in the
  world: a painted UI card with green corner blobs on a flat slab. It is now a steel floor plate with a hazard-striped
  rim and four bolts (mesh `Tiles/PurchaseBase`, the machines' hazard material), carrying a plain rounded panel whose
  colour is the state: navy = saving up, green = affordable, grey = locked. Name on top, icon in the middle, price
  below, pay fill over the panel. Anything on the ground that the player stands on should use this language (plate,
  hazard rim) or the pads' (outlined rectangle), not a card. The upgrade tile and the boost pad still have their old
  look.
- **Claw cranes** (`Crane_Build.BuildCrane`, meshes `Mach_ClawCrane`), hero pass in Revamp 4: concrete foundation, a
  lattice mast (four posts with cross lacing and a ladder strip), an operator's cab with glass beside the jib, a
  counter-jib with a walkway and three counterweight blocks, a tower head with tie rods, a triangular truss jib in
  three sections with a red end plate each (a level adds a section), a four-wheel trolley, twin cables, a pulley
  block and a red four-finger claw. The Heavy Yard crane is the same rig on a taller mast (7.6 m against 5.75 m).

## Stalls without a roof (2026-10-06)

The Sell Desk and Metal Market lost their striped canopy and tall posts (`Art_Machines.SellDesk`): from the game camera
the roof hid the counter's stack and the stock pad. The stall is an open counter with a striped front skirt, two short

## Environment pass (2026-10-06, `Env_Build`, ProBuilder)

The owner asked for the environment to be improved with ProBuilder. `Env_Build` builds only ProBuilder meshes (cubes
with bevelled edges, cylinders), merged per cluster, in the toy materials (`M_Env*` in `Art/Materials/Toy`, made with
`ArtMaterials.Mat` so they carry the same wear detail as the ArtKit models). They stay editable in the scene.

- **Lanes**: dark asphalt strips with yellow edge lines and white dashes, only on bare ground (never across a
  concrete floor). They mark where to walk and keep clutter and spawn points out.
- **Bays**: an oil-dark slab with yellow corner marks under every heavy vehicle. The Heavy Scrap Yard reads as a
  breaker's yard: rows of parked wrecks between lanes, not eight vehicles on sand.
- **Clusters**: scrap bales, pipe stacks, concrete barriers, drums, crates on pallets; tanks and floodlight towers
  only along north walls. Islands in open ground are always low (about 1.1 m): the camera looks north, and anything
  tall south of a walkway hides the player.
- Placement is by measurement, not by eye: a cluster is built only where its footprint (plus walking room) is free of
  every existing mesh, tile, pad, spawn point, lane and the giant's landing zone. Adding decor elsewhere first and
  re-running `Env_Build.All` is safe.

**Old Yard reference look** (`Env_Build.OldYard`): the owner chose to bring one area to the target look first and
roll it out after approval. The yard's sand became warm brick paving and the scrap field a dark gravel pit (generated
textures `T_GroundPavers`, `T_GroundGravel`; one material per ground slab because the repeat depends on its size).
The pit has a yellow / dark hazard kerb with bollards at its three openings, and a lit SCRAP YARD KING billboard
stands above the north wall. The first version also painted two asphalt lanes with the words SCRAP and SELL across
the yard; the owner rejected them (a road under the light pole and the debris, text overlapping props), so the Old
Yard has no painted lanes or floor words. Lesson: do not lay new ground graphics across props that are already there. Still the old look in the Old Yard: the
walls, the machines, the office and props, the Back Lot's ground.

Not done in this pass: every other area still has one sand texture, the walls are unchanged, the plant and
the Furnace hall got almost nothing (their walls are already lined with heaps and props), and nothing was built at
the Dockyard. No frame-time measurement was taken after adding the clusters and 13 more scrap objects.

## Remaining / risks

- Characters keep 2-segment rounding for the hero look; a LOD (chamfer-only) version would halve their cost on
  low-end devices.
- Scrap objects stay multi-renderer (detachable parts); about 10-12 draw calls per car with SRP batching.
- SSAO is on the PC renderer; check the Mobile renderer asset before shipping (quality setting per device tier).
- Ambient traffic and birds are presentation only and ignore workers; keep their routes off gameplay paths. Road
  lanes: traffic at z 4.0 and 6.5, the Truck Dock's truck on the north shoulder at z 9.1.
- The camera looks north from above and never turns. Anything tall south of a walkway covers the player: on the quay
  the cranes, the ship and the three-high stacks all stand north of the lane, and the south stacks are one or two high.
- The Giant Truck is 10.5 × 5.6 × 6.7 m. It fits a portrait screen side-on only with the fight camera's pull-back
  (`GiantEventConfig.cameraPullBack`); a bigger giant needs more pull-back or a different facing.
- The kit textures are 1536 × 1024 paintings. The upgrade panel and the purchase tiles stretch their sprites to about
  twice the painted size; it holds up at 1080 wide, a tablet would want a higher-resolution export of Atlas2.
- The guide arrow, the joystick, the boost gauge ring and the pay fill on tiles are still the plain generated shapes
  (they rotate, fill radially or get tinted every frame, which painted sprites do not survive).
- The painted loading background shows machines and a yard that are richer than the in-game models. It sets the
  mood; the first seconds of play should not feel like a step down (worth a look on a device).
- The Dockyard has its prop set now (ship, quay cranes, bollards). A Press hall and a loading berth would reuse the
  machine kit from `Art_Machines` and the `TruckBay` dock pieces.
